Most traders can’t outperform because they take emotional or isolated decisions without understanding second and third-order effects.

Myths & Generic Rules That Limit Traders

Myth: “Never let a good gain turn into a loss”

Leads to premature profit-taking. Stock goes up 10%, you book. If it pulls back to 5%, you still profit — but miss the 200% move Zanger would hold.

Myth: “You can never go broke taking a profit”

Nicholas Darvas challenged this directly. If a stock is growing, why take profit? The remaining position has no life-changing impact.

Myth: “Always sell into strength”

This is one way, not THE way. Zanger held through climactic moves to capture 75-100% gains. Selling into strength would have cut returns dramatically.

Myth: “Find what suits you”

“What suits you” = what feels comfortable. Comfort leads to mediocre outcomes. It’s about where you want to reach, not what feels safe.

The Trap: You validate isolated rules from Minervini/others without context, leading to constant confusion, regrades, and frustration — a treadmill where you walk but never arrive.

Stock Splits as Catalyst

“Nothing more enjoyable than a stock split in a hot stock in a hot group.”

— Dan Zanger

The Split Effect

Stock splits attract retail investors who perceive the “lower price” as a bargain. This creates a fresh wave of buying demand.

Timing Strategy

Buy BEFORE the split announcement when the stock is already running. Sell into the post-split euphoria run. The split creates an acceleration event.

DCLK Example

DCLK announced a 2:1 split. The stock jumped $24 in a single day to $131. This parabolic move was the exit signal for Zanger.

Key Lesson: Splits are not fundamentally meaningful, but they are psychologically powerful catalysts. In a hot market with a hot stock, a split announcement can trigger 20-30% moves in days.

Base Formation Requirements

Zanger required stocks to form a proper base before buying. No base = no buy. Period.

Base Characteristics

Deep Corrections Are OK

  • Depth matters less than the base structure
  • What matters: is the stock in the RIGHT sector?

Important Distinction: Zanger’s bases were often “ugly” by Minervini/O’Neil standards. No VCPs, no tight patterns. But the STOCK SELECTION was so good that imperfect setups still worked brilliantly.

Trend Line Breaks: The Primary Entry Signal

How Zanger Used Trend Lines

  • Draw diagonal descending trend line connecting lower highs during the base
  • Wait for price to break ABOVE the trend line on increased volume
  • Break = base maturity confirmed. This is the BUY signal.
  • Buy ON the break, not in anticipation of the break

What NOT to Do

  • Don’t buy in ANTICIPATION of the breakout
  • Don’t wait for confirmation candles (too late)
  • Don’t obsess about the “perfect pivot point”
  • Don’t use horizontal pivots only — trend lines are more flexible

Why Trend Lines? Trend line breaks capture the MOMENT supply is exhausted and demand takes over. This is a more organic signal than a fixed pivot price. Zanger used this as his primary tool for 29 months.

The 15–20 Minute Rule

Zanger NEVER bought in the first 15–20 minutes of the trading day. This single rule saved him from countless fake breakouts.

0–15 min

Retail frenzy. Fake breakouts. Emotional orders hitting the market.

15–45 min

Volume cooloff period. Check if breakout holds or fades.

45+ min

If breakout sustains with volume, it’s a REAL move. BUY now.

Safety Net

Avoid initial sellers on sell side too. Wait for dust to settle.

Why the First 15 Minutes Are Dangerous

Retail Crowd: Emotional buyers who saw the stock gap up and rush in. They create the initial volume spike.
Market Makers: Widen spreads and take advantage of emotional orders. The bid-ask spread is at its widest.
Smart Money: Waits for the retail dust to settle. Tests the breakout by letting it pull back. Then buys the dip.

Trailing Stop Method: Rising Trend Line + 5% Buffer

How It Works

1
As stock rises, draw a rising trend line along the higher lows
2
Add a 5% buffer below the rising trend line
3
If stock drops 5% below the rising trend line = SELL

Key Characteristics

  • Not a precise mathematical trail
  • Organic — follows the stock’s natural movement
  • Gives the stock room to breathe on pullbacks
  • Prevents buying too high into extended moves

Zanger’s Wisdom: “Staying close to the trend line is controlling your emotions from buying too high.” The rising trend line serves dual purpose — it defines the exit AND prevents chasing stocks at extended prices.
