## The story the numbers tell  (Evidence → Interpretation → Conclusion)

- **Evidence:** Net profit **peaked at ₹69,644M in FY17, then collapsed ~70% to ₹20,957M in FY18**,
  with revenue also *falling* FY17→FY18 (₹315,784M → ₹264,895M). R&D % *rose* to 8.6% in FY18.
- **Interpretation:** This is the **Ranbaxy-integration + Halol-remediation + US-generic-price-erosion
  trough** all hitting at once — Sun over-earned on the Ranbaxy/gleevec window, then US pricing collapsed
  and the Halol warning letter throttled US supply, while R&D spend stayed high.
- **Conclusion:** Sun's history *is* the sector thesis in one company — **commodity US generics nearly
  broke the leader (FY18), and the recovery to FY25's ₹109bn profit was built by pivoting to Global
  Specialty.** The current ₹3.1bn net cash + 20%-of-sales specialty is the *answer* to the FY18 problem.
  **[H]**

## Capital-allocation read (Module 10)
- **Net worth compounded** from ₹148,862M (FY13) to ₹477,713M (FY22) — strong retained-earnings build,
  conservative balance sheet (Sun has long run net cash). **[H, AR FY21-22]**
- **R&D discipline:** spend held ~6–8% of sales through the cycle — *raised* into the FY18 trough
  (counter-cyclical, building the specialty pipeline that drove the recovery). **[H]**
- **Inorganic:** Taro (2010, value-creating specialty engine), Ranbaxy (2014, $3.2bn — the painful
  FY18 trough but eventually digested for scale), Checkpoint Therapeutics (FY25, for Unloxcyt). [S: AR + FY25 release]
- **Dividend:** raised to ₹16.0/share for FY25 (vs ₹13.5 FY24). [S: Sun Q4FY25 release]

- US: **542 ANDAs approved, 117 pending** (33 tentative); US sales $1,921M (+3.6%).
- R&D ₹32,484M (6.2% of sales).

## The live risk (Module 5c / 14)
**Halol = OAI, import ban to the US (ex-shortage)**, and **failed a re-inspection in Jun-2025**
[S: STAT/ProPublica, 2025]. This is the single biggest swing factor: a clearance lifts the compliance
score (3→4/5) and likely retakes the #1 rubric rank from Cipla.

## Bear case (steelman)
Specialty R&D is cash-hungry and binary; the FY18 episode shows how fast US generics can de-rate the
whole company; Halol could escalate to a broader import alert; at scale, India-franchise growth alone
can't move a $6bn-revenue company — specialty *must* deliver. **[M]**
