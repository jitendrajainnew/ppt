Premier), a solar-glass component maker (Borosil Renewables), and two IPP/developer-utilities

## What the long-run data reveals (and how it confirms `solar.md`)

1. **Evidence:** Waaree sales went ₹1,996cr→₹26,537cr (~13×) with OPM 5%→22% and ROCE 16%→39%;
   Premier ₹947cr→₹7,824cr (~8×), turned profitable FY24, ROCE to 33-41%.
   **Interpretation:** Indian *module/cell manufacturing* re-rated from thin-margin assembly to a
   high-return business as PLI incentives + ALMM/import duties + a domestic-content build-out hit at
   once — operating leverage on scale shows up as *both* margin expansion and ROCE expansion together.
   **Conclusion:** The data confirms the manufacturing-up-cycle leg of solar.md's value-chain map —
   module makers are *currently* earning the highest returns in the chain. **[H]**

2. **Evidence:** Borosil Renewables (solar glass) lost money FY24-25 (-₹50cr, -₹87cr; ROCE -4%) then
   recovered hard in FY26 (OPM 4%→28%, profit ₹127cr, ROCE 25%).
   **Interpretation:** A *commodity component* one step up the chain got squeezed by Chinese glass
   dumping, then recovered once anti-dumping protection and pricing turned. This is the
   commoditisation/cycle risk the framework warns the manufacturing layer carries.
   **Conclusion:** Borosil is the live counter-example — it shows the manufacturing-margin up-cycle is
   *not* permanent; the component closest to commodity glass already lived a full down-cycle. **[H]**

3. **Evidence:** Adani Green runs ~57-83% OPM (recurring PPA cash flows) yet ROCE only 7-10%; interest
   (₹6,484cr FY26ttm) nearly equals operating profit's contribution to PBT, and PBT stayed *negative*
   through FY21.
   **Interpretation:** The IPP model is high-margin-but-low-ROCE by construction — heavy upfront,
   debt-funded capex against long contracted tariffs. Margin tells you the *asset* is good; ROCE tells
   you the *capital structure* eats most of it.
   **Conclusion:** Confirms solar.md's IPP characterisation — developers are leveraged yield plays, not
   high-return compounders; judge them on cash yield and balance sheet, not on headline OPM. **[H]**

4. **Evidence:** Tata Power sits at a steady ~11% ROCE, OPM ~21-23%, sales ₹29kcr→₹62kcr — no swings.
   **Interpretation:** An integrated utility (generation + T&D + retail + a solar arm) averages out the
   cycle; it neither rides the manufacturing up-cycle nor lives the IPP leverage extreme.
   **Conclusion:** The "boring middle" of the chain — confirms the integrated-utility profile as the
   low-volatility, modest-return anchor. **[M]**

5. **The variant / falsification to watch — manufacturing-margin *sustainability*.** Waaree/Premier's
   20-30% OPM and 35-40% ROCE are *up-cycle* numbers, earned while domestic supply was short and
   protected. Borosil's glass cycle is the warning shot from one rung up. **If** Indian module capacity
   over-builds (as global modules already did) **and** duties/PLI ease, manufacturing margins could
   compress toward Borosil's FY24-25 trough rather than hold. The data confirms the *current* margin
   map; it does **not** prove the manufacturers' high returns are structural. That is the open question
   the scorecard must price. **[H — the risk is well-evidenced; the outcome is not yet decided]**

## Net read on the value-chain margin map

- **Modules/manufacturing (Waaree, Premier):** highest *current* ROCE in the chain — the up-cycle is
  real and data-confirmed, but treat the 35-40% as cyclical-peak, not steady-state.
- **Components/solar glass (Borosil):** the chain's most commoditised rung; already showed a full
  loss-and-recovery cycle — the canary for what over-supply does to manufacturing returns.
- **IPPs/developers (Adani Green):** high OPM, low ROCE, heavily levered — yield plays, balance-sheet-
  dependent.
- **Integrated utility (Tata Power):** steady ~11% ROCE — the low-volatility anchor.
