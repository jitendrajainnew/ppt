## What the long-run data reveals

**1. The yield-instrument signature: very high OPM, very low and flat ROCE.**
- *Evidence:* IndiGrid OPM sits 85–96% across FY18–FY25 (availability-based transmission — it gets
  paid for being available, not for volume), yet ROCE never leaves the 7–11% band. IRB InvIT shows
  the same shape one notch lower (~80% OPM, 5–9% ROCE).
- *Interpretation:* The huge margin is the asset quality (regulated/contracted cash flow with tiny
  cash opex); the low ROCE is the **leverage + concession amortisation** that defines an InvIT. These
  are leveraged *yield vehicles*, not high-return *compounders*. A 90% margin tells you the asset is
  good; the 8% ROCE tells you the unitholder's return comes from distribution yield, not retained
  compounding.
- *Conclusion:* The data **confirms invits.md** — judging an InvIT on ROCE/EPS is the wrong lens.
  IndiGrid's high, stable margin is the availability-based quality signature you *want*. **[H]**

**2. IndiGrid = the stable availability-based core; IRB InvIT carries traffic risk.**
- *Evidence:* IndiGrid's OPM is rock-steady (85–96%) and Sales grew 448→3,288cr (FY18→FY25) via
  acquisitions. IRB InvIT's Sales are choppier (992→1,442→1,062→1,084→1,549) and its margin took a
  one-off hit in FY23 (56%).
- *Interpretation:* Transmission revenue is **availability-based** (paid on uptime, traffic-agnostic),
  so IndiGrid's top line is contractual and predictable. Toll-road revenue is **traffic-based**, so
  IRB InvIT's Sales wobble with volumes, concession resets and asset additions/expiries.
- *Conclusion:* IndiGrid = lower-risk, contracted yield; IRB InvIT = higher-risk, traffic-exposed
  yield. Same instrument class, different underlying-cash-flow risk — exactly the distinction the
  thesis draws. **[H]**

**3. PGInvIT's GAAP volatility is the proof that you ignore reported profit.**
- *Evidence:* On essentially FLAT revenue (~₹1,217–1,286cr every year, FY22–FY26), PGInvIT's Net
  Profit went ₹463cr → **-₹446cr (FY23)** → ₹982cr → ₹1,172cr (FY25) → ₹912cr, EPS swung from
  -₹2.14 to ₹12.92, and OPM% printed -24% then 133%.
- *Interpretation:* Nothing operationally changed — the same transmission assets earned the same
  availability fee. The GAAP profit swing is **depreciation/amortisation and one-off accounting on a
  finite-life concession**. Concession assets are written down over their life, so reported earnings
  are dominated by non-cash, schedule-driven charges that have no bearing on distributable cash.
- *Conclusion:* This is the textbook case for why InvITs are valued on **NDCF / DPU**, not EPS or
  ROCE. If you'd sold PGInvIT on its FY23 loss you'd have missed an asset still throwing off stable
  cash. The data **confirms** the "GAAP earnings are the wrong meter" claim. **[H]**

**4. The right scorecard: NDCF coverage + DPU sustainability + yield-vs-G-sec spread.**
- *Evidence:* Across all three trusts, the *stable* lines are Sales and Operating Profit (IndiGrid
  OP 408→3,062cr, IRB OP ~₹800–1,240cr, PGInvIT OP stable ex-FY23); the *unstable* lines are Net
  Profit, EPS and ROCE. Dividend payout ratios routinely exceed 100% (IndiGrid 86–416%, IRB
  124–1517%, PGInvIT 93–189%) — distributions exceed GAAP profit because they include
  **return-of-capital**.
- *Interpretation:* Payout >100% of GAAP profit isn't a red flag here — it's structural. An InvIT
  distributes NDCF, which includes amortisation/return-of-capital, so distributions routinely run
  above accounting profit. That is precisely why "yield ≠ income": part of every distribution is your
  own capital coming back over the finite concession life.
- *Conclusion:* Judge these on **NDCF coverage of DPU**, **DPU sustainability over remaining
  concession life**, and the **distribution-yield-vs-G-sec spread** — not on EPS or ROCE. The data
  operationally validates the invits.md framing end-to-end. **[H]**

## Bottom line

The 10-year series confirms the thesis: InvITs are **finite-life concession yield instruments**, not
compounders. Very high OPM (IndiGrid 85–96%, IRB ~80%) signals contracted/availability asset quality;
low, flat ROCE (~5–9%) is the leverage + concession-amortisation signature; PGInvIT's GAAP swing
(-₹446cr → ₹1,172cr on flat revenue) is the live demonstration that EPS/ROCE are distorted by
concession accounting. Value them on **NDCF coverage + DPU sustainability + yield-vs-G-sec spread**,
and remember **yield ≠ income** — part of the distribution is return-of-capital. **[H]**
