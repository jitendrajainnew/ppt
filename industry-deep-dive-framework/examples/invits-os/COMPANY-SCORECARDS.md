# InvIT Scorecards — one rubric, every trust

> One **InvIT-specific weighted rubric** (derived from `../invits.md` Module 11) applied to every trust.
> Scores are analyst judgment shown *with the evidence*, built from the report's DPU/AUM/leverage data
> and the **real 10Y operating series** in `FINANCIALS-10Y.md` [S: Screener.in structured tables,
> ~June 2026]. ⚠️ Cross-check vs trust NDCF/DPU disclosures, valuer reports and rating rationales before
> real use. **Not investment advice.**
>
> **⚠️ EPS / ROCE are the WRONG lens here — and the rubric encodes that.** An InvIT is a finite-life
> concession **yield instrument**, not a retained-earnings compounder. GAAP profit is dominated by
> non-cash concession amortisation: **PGInvIT swung −₹446cr (FY23) → +₹1,172cr (FY25) on flat ~₹1,260cr
> revenue**; IndiGrid earns an **85–96% OPM yet only ~7–8% ROCE** (the gap is leverage + amortisation,
> not weakness). **None of the six rubric factors below is EPS or ROCE** — they are asset-risk, leverage,
> *cash*-distribution sustainability, pipeline, accretion and governance. **[H]**

## The rubric (weights — from invits.md Module 11)

| # | Factor | Weight | "5/5" |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Asset-type / demand-risk** | **25%** | Availability/annuity assets (≈99% availability, no volume risk), long replenished concession life |
| 2 | **Leverage / coverage** | **20%** | Net debt/AUM comfortably under 49/70% caps, AAA, NDCF covers debt service |
| 3 | **Distribution sustainability (cash vs return-of-capital)** | **20%** | NDCF covers DPU from *cash*; low return-of-capital share; DPU growing |
| 4 | **Sponsor pipeline + alignment** | **15%** | Deep ROFO + third-party pipeline; low sponsor-conflict; manager priced for unitholders |
| 5 | **Accretion track record** | **12%** | Serial accretive buys (asset yield-on-cost > cost of capital), DPU accretion per unit |
| 6 | **Governance** | **8%** | Clean related-party / valuation discipline, unitholder rights |
|   | **Total** | **100%** | |

**Scale:** 1 = red flag · 3 = average · 5 = best-in-class. **"Good" = availability/annuity assets +
disciplined leverage (AAA) + cash-backed growing DPU + deep accretive pipeline + clean governance.**

## Reasoning per trust (Evidence → Interpretation → Conclusion)

### IndiGrid — the AAA availability compounder (4.07/5, Rank 1)
- **Asset-risk = 5:** transmission, **availability-based tariff (~99% availability)** → near-zero demand
  risk; Sales grew **448→3,288cr (FY18→FY25)** with OPM rock-steady **85–96%** = contracted asset quality.
- **Leverage/coverage = 4:** net debt/AUM ≈59% (within the 70% cap), **AAA, cost of debt ≈7.74% (lowest
  in class)** — high but availability-cash-consistent.
- **Distribution sustainability = 4:** **DPU ₹15.0→₹16.0 guide (+6.7%), cash-funded**, NDCF ~33% CAGR
  FY20–24; low–med return-of-capital share. Not a 5 only because leverage funds part of the growth.
- **Pipeline/align = 4:** sponsor ROFO **+** active third-party transmission deal flow off the grid-capex
  super-cycle; externally KKR-managed (the alignment watch).
- **Accretion = 4:** serial accretive buyer (sponsor drop-downs + third-party) at a positive spread; the
  renewables foray is the "mixed" watch-item.
- **Governance = 3:** externally managed + sponsor-conflict on drop-down pricing.
- **Conclusion:** the *good* InvIT archetype — **cash-covered, growing, AAA, availability cash flows.** **[H]**

### PowerGrid InvIT — low-leverage but coverage-watch (4.07/5, ranked aside, low growth)
- **Asset-risk = 5:** transmission, availability-based — same near-zero-demand-risk quality as IndiGrid.
- **Leverage/coverage = 5:** **net debt/AUM ≈0.26% — essentially unlevered**, the most balance-sheet
  headroom in the class.
- **Distribution sustainability = 4 (with a flag):** **held ₹12 DPU FY25 partly by dipping into reserves**
  — the payout is not fully cash-covered, so it does *not* earn a 5 despite the pristine sponsor.
- **Pipeline/align = 3:** pristine PGCIL sponsor but **limited / slow drop-down pace** = shallow runway.
- **Accretion = 2:** the weak link — **near-unlevered and barely acquiring**, so DPU is flat with no
  growth engine (huge unused leverage headroom going unused).
- **Governance = 4:** PGCIL/clean structure.
- **Conclusion:** the **low-risk-but-no-growth bond-proxy with a coverage warning.** Its GAAP profit swing
  (−446→1,172 on flat revenue) is the live proof you must value it on **NDCF, not EPS/ROCE.** **[H]**

### IRB InvIT — toll/traffic risk, declining DPU (2.71/5, Rank 2)
- **Asset-risk = 2:** **roads (BOT toll + 1 HAM) = full traffic/volume risk on top of a ticking concession
  clock**; Sales choppy (992→1,442→1,062→1,084→1,549) tracking volumes/concession resets — the opposite of
  availability quality.
- **Leverage/coverage = 3:** ~61% net debt/AUM, AA+/AAA — average, not a cushion.
- **Distribution sustainability = 3:** **DPU FELL ₹10.55 (FY18) → ₹8.0 (FY24)**, guided to ₹6.3–6.5 FY27;
  the ~12%+ headline yield is **toll-amortisation + traffic-risk heavy = high return-of-capital** (your own
  money back), and GAAP net profit (356) sits far below the distribution.
- **Pipeline/align = 3:** asset-specific, finite concessions.
- **Accretion = 3:** episodic; finite-life base needs replacement it isn't clearly getting.
- **Governance = 3:** externally managed (IRB).
- **Conclusion:** the **high-headline-yield trap** — yield ≠ income; a declining-DPU depreciating-asset
  vehicle. **[H]**

## ⚠️ What the rubric encodes that EPS/ROCE would get BACKWARDS

1. **Headline yield is *inversely* related to durability.** IRB's ~12%+ yield scores **lowest** (2.71);
   IndiGrid's lower ~9–10% yield scores **highest** (4.07). An EPS/ROCE screen would miss this entirely —
   IRB's ROCE (~9%) actually *exceeds* IndiGrid's (~8%). **The rubric correctly inverts it. [H]**
2. **PGInvIT's GAAP profit is meaningless.** −₹446cr (FY23) → +₹1,172cr (FY25) on flat revenue; a
   profit-based screen would have flagged it a falling knife in FY23 and a star in FY25 — both wrong. The
   rubric scores it on **asset-risk + leverage + coverage**, landing it correctly as safe-but-no-growth.
3. **High OPM + low ROCE is the *signature*, not a flaw.** IndiGrid's 90%-OPM/8%-ROCE gap is leverage +
   concession amortisation — exactly what you *want* in an availability asset; penalising the low ROCE
   would penalise the best trust.

## Re-grade triggers
- **IndiGrid → 4.3+ / clear #1:** if NDCF coverage confirms the ₹16 FY26 DPU is fully *cash*-funded and the
  renewables foray stays small (asset-risk stays 5).
- **PGInvIT → genuine #1 contender:** if it deploys its ~0.26% leverage into accretive PGCIL drops
  (accretion 2→4) *and* covers DPU from cash (sustainability 4→5).
- **IRB → re-rate up:** only on a visible *cash*-coverage / traffic inflection that arrests the DPU decline.
- **Any trust → downgrade:** DPU sustained only by rising leverage or return-of-capital (coverage <1x),
  sub-cost-of-capital deals, or a sponsor governance/pledge event.
