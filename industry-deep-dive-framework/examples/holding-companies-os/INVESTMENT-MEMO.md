## THE VARIANT VIEWS — where 80% of the return is

### Variant 1 — the discount is *conditional*, not *permanent* (the core call)
- **Consensus:** "Holdco discounts are **permanent traps** — structural dead money, always avoid."
- **Variant [M]:** The discount is **conditional**. Specific, **nameable catalysts DO narrow it**:
  **(1) buyback at a discount** (≈2× accretive/₹), **(2) demerger / collapse of the holding structure**
  (hands underlying shares to holders, discount → ~0), **(3) delisting** (SEBI reverse-book-build),
  **(4) dividend-policy step-up**, **(5) activist pressure**, **(6) tax-law change** (DDT-abolition done;
  buyback-tax shift live). **Berkshire** — a holdco trading *at/above* book — proves the discount is
  not an iron law. **Own the discount ONLY where a catalyst is credible.**
- **Evidence:** DDT abolition (2020) removed a structural leg; episodic demergers/delistings *have*
  re-rated specific names; real look-through data confirms the **NAV-compounding leg** (Bajaj ~15%, Tata ~8%).
- **Steelman the bears:** promoters run these for **control**, not minorities — so they rarely fire the
  catalyst voluntarily. **Neither Bajaj nor Tata has run a buyback** despite deep discounts; floats are
  tiny; substitutes exist (just buy Bajaj Auto directly); most names have stayed cheap for *decades*.
  **The base rate strongly favours the bears.**

### Variant 2 — ROCE makes the whole category *look* uninvestable, and that's the inefficiency
- **Consensus:** Holdcos earn 1–13% ROCE → low-quality → skip.
- **Variant [H]:** ROCE is **mechanically depressed** by cost/equity-method accounting (gains unrealised,
  cost base stale). The same companies compound look-through earnings at **~8–15%**. The screen-rejection
  *is* the mispricing — value the NAV, not the ratio.
- **Falsified if:** look-through Net Profit *stalls/declines* over multiple years (then NAV genuinely isn't compounding and the cheapness is deserved).

### Variant 3 — Bajaj is a *discounted compounder*, not yet a *special situation*
- **Consensus:** "Bajaj Holdings is the obvious cheap holdco re-rating play."
- **Variant [M]:** It is the best *asset* (Auto+Finserv, net cash, ~15% NAV CAGR) but the **catalyst is
  absent** — no buyback, control-aligned promoter. So today it is a **levered-by-discount proxy** on
  Bajaj Auto/Finserv — fine if you're happy owning them *via* a permanent discount, but *strictly worse
  than owning them directly* unless the discount narrows. Buy it as a compounder; only *upsize* on a fired catalyst.
- **Falsified if:** Bajaj announces a real buyback/demerger → it becomes a genuine special situation.

## FALSIFICATION TEST — my variant is proven WRONG if:
**(a)** a holdco runs a **deep buyback or sharply lifts payout** and the discount **still** fails to
narrow over **4–6 quarters**; **or (b)** a **demerger/structure-collapse** completes and the combined
value **still** trades at a deep discount; **or (c)** post-2020 tax changes produce **no measurable
narrowing** across the class over multiple years. *Data caveat:* the 10-yr P&L confirms NAV compounding
but is **silent on the discount** (a price-vs-NAV series not in the tables) — **sourcing the discount
time-series is the explicit next step** before sizing the re-rating leg. **[M]**

|---|---|---|
| **Special situation** | **Yes — the native fit** | When a **specific catalyst** (buyback / demerger / delisting) is visible and dated; **buyback-at-discount is THE unlock** — size to the catalyst, not the NAV |

## The one-line thesis
**Holdco discounts are conditional, not permanent — own them only where (a) the underlying compounds NAV
(Bajaj ~15%, Tata ~8% look-through, REAL data) and (b) a credible catalyst — above all a buyback at the
discount — is visible. Bajaj Holdings is the best asset but, lacking a buyback, is a discounted
compounder not yet a special situation; the unlock is the buyback-at-discount, and its absence is exactly
why the gap persists.** *(Answers the Research Question: NAV-compounding leg = confirmed by data;
discount-narrowing leg = unconfirmed, awaiting a catalyst. Not investment advice.)*
