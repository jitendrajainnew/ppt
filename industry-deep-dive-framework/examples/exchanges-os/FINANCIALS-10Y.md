## What the long-run data reveals

**1. IEX is the purest toll-road — structurally ~84% OPM, ROCE 50–61%. [H]**
- *Evidence:* OPM has sat at 79–84% every year since listing (FY19), printing 84% for five straight years (FY22→26); ROCE ran 56→61→50→53→51%. Sales 254→537cr while expenses crept 51→84cr.
- *Interpretation:* The matching engine is built once and scaled at near-zero marginal cost — incremental volume drops almost entirely to operating profit. That is the network-effect "toll-road" in its cleanest form: utility-like volumes earned at software-like margins.
- *Conclusion:* IEX is the textbook proof of the thesis — the highest, most *stable* margin in the set. Stability, not peak, is the moat tell.

**2. CDSL — the operating-leverage compounder; ROCE re-rated 14%→42%. [H]**
- *Evidence:* Sales compounded ~26% (105→1,082cr) while OPM expanded 43%→58% (peaking 66% in FY22); ROCE climbed 14% (FY15) → 40–42% (FY22–25). Zero interest cost throughout, dividend payout 50–58%.
- *Interpretation:* A duopoly depository (with NSDL) charging per-account / per-transaction tolls. As demat accounts and traded volume grew, a near-fixed cost base turned every rupee of incremental revenue into margin — and excess cash into dividends, not capex.
- *Conclusion:* Confirms the platform thesis. **Risk flag:** FY26ttm shows the cyclicality — sales 1,082→1,145cr but profit *fell* 526→455cr and ROCE slipped 42%→32%, reflecting softer capital-market volumes and regulatory/fee pressure. The toll-road is real, but the traffic is sentiment-cyclical. **[H]**

**3. CAMS — the "picks-and-shovels" monopoly RTA; ROCE 38–56%. [H]**
- *Evidence:* ROCE held 38–56% across FY19–26 (54% in FY25); OPM rose 37%→46–47%; sales 659→1,422cr (~11.6% CAGR) with profit 146→465cr.
- *Interpretation:* CAMS services ~two-thirds of Indian MF AUM as registrar — an entrenched, switching-cost-heavy back office that earns a basis-point toll on a structurally rising AUM pool. Lower OPM than IEX/CDSL because it is more operations- and people-intensive, but returns on capital are still exceptional.
- *Conclusion:* Validates the thesis with the most *AUM-linked* (vs trade-volume-linked) revenue — arguably the steadiest demand driver, even if margins sit below the pure exchanges.

**4. BSE & MCX — same toll-road economics, but the swings expose the cyclicality. [M]**
- *Evidence:* BSE ROCE swung 5% (FY20) → 47% (FY25) → 58% (ttm) as derivatives volumes exploded — profit 121→1,322→2,487cr, OPM 21%→64%. MCX cratered to 9% OPM / 7% ROCE in FY24 (a one-off tech-platform migration cost spike, expenses 514→621cr on flat sales) then snapped to 60–71% OPM and 43–71% ROCE once the new platform went live.
- *Interpretation:* When volume arrives, the fixed-cost rail prints extraordinary incremental margins (BSE/MCX FY25–26). When it doesn't — or when a one-time cost hits — the same operating leverage works in reverse (MCX FY24).
- *Conclusion:* These prove the *upside* of the toll-road but also its *operating leverage in both directions*. Higher beta to volumes than IEX/CDSL/CAMS.

**5. Who has the most durable moat?**
- *Evidence:* Ranked by margin *stability*: IEX (OPM 79–84%, ROCE 50–61%) > CAMS (OPM 37–47%, ROCE 38–56%, AUM-linked) > CDSL (OPM 40–66%, ROCE 14→42%, account-linked) > BSE/MCX (OPM 9–64%, ROCE 5–71%, trade-volume-linked and cost-event-prone).
- *Interpretation:* Durability tracks how *volume-/sentiment-cyclical* the revenue is. IEX's regulated single-platform position and CAMS's AUM-linked annuity are steadier than trade-count-driven BSE/MCX. The shared risk across all five: regulation can cap take-rates (SEBI/CERC fee caps), and volumes follow market sentiment — the very toll that makes them monopolies is the toll a regulator can compress (tie to `exchanges.md`).
- *Conclusion:* **The data PROVES the Network/Platform "toll-road" thesis** — near-monopoly market infrastructure earns utility-like volumes at software-like margins (OPM 40–84%, ROCE routinely 40–60%+, near-zero debt, high payouts). **Most durable: IEX, then CAMS.** Highest upside-leverage but most cyclical: BSE/MCX. The standing risk is regulatory take-rate caps + volume cyclicality (visible in CDSL's FY26ttm dip). **[H]**
