## THE VARIANT VIEWS — where 80% of the return is  [CORE]

### Variant 1 — Indian steel is NOT just a perennial China-hostage value-trap
- **Consensus:** "Indian steel = cheap, perennially-cyclical China-hostage value-destroyer; trade the
  spread, never own it."
- **Variant [M]:** India is a **genuine multi-decade per-capita demand story** (≈100kg/capita vs world
  ≈230, China ≈600+), and **post-IBC consolidation + captive-ore auctions raised the through-cycle
  EBITDA/tonne floor** under the cost-curve winners. The cycle is real, but the **floor is structurally
  higher than last cycle** — *own the integrated, captive-ore, net-debt-disciplined, value-added
  leader, not the commodity tail.*
- **Evidence:** the real data — JSW's trough OPM held ~13–16% and JSPL's ~20% (vs SAIL/Tata halving);
  IBC handed Bhushan/Essar/Monnet to disciplined hands; per-capita gap intact. **[M]**
- **★ Falsified if:** China sustains an export flood with **no safeguard/anti-dumping response** and
  domestic spreads stay at/under trough for **4+ quarters**, **OR** the top names add **leveraged
  capacity at the price peak** (net-debt/EBITDA >3x mid-build), **OR** coking coal stays structurally
  elevated and crushes through-cycle spreads, **OR** per-capita growth stalls (back-to-back sub-trend
  demand prints).

### Variant 2 — the "best name" matters less than "when you buy"
- **Consensus:** Pick the highest-quality steel franchise and hold it.
- **Variant [H]:** The real 10-yr data is **unambiguous — every name's returns are dominated by *where
  in the cycle you buy*, not which name you pick.** Tata: ROCE 1%→31%→7%, profit −₹4,169cr→+₹41,749cr→
  −₹4,910cr. **No structural compounder exists** — even JSW ran 5%→28%→8%. The alpha is **entry
  discipline**: buy into trough losses, sell into peak earnings. **[H]**
- **Evidence:** the ~30-point ROCE swing and ₹46,000cr profit swing in Tata's single-cycle row;
  all four names print trough-loss → peak-profit on the same schedule.
- **Falsified if:** a name sustains double-digit ROCE *through* a full trough (a genuine
  compounder emerges) — the data shows none has yet.

### Variant 3 — JSW's "great operator" status doesn't fix its cost-curve gap (contrarian-to-the-bull)
- **Consensus:** JSW is the best steelmaker, so it's the safest long.
- **Variant [M]:** JSW is a **superb operator on a commodity with thin moats** — but it is **ore-short
  (≈15–20% captive)**, so a captive-ore peer (Tata/SAIL) out-earns it ≈₹2,000–4,000/t at every peak;
  its **≈38 MTPA capex is being built into a China-dumping spread**. *Execution doesn't fix a
  cost-curve gap, and demand doesn't protect the spread from China.* Prefer it as the **mildest-drawdown
  cyclical hold**, but don't pay peak-earnings multiples for it. **[M]**
- **Evidence:** the C1=3 cost-curve gap in the scorecard; ~2–3x net-debt/EBITDA on growth capex `[verify]`.
- **Falsified if:** JSW wins captive-ore blocks closing the gap (C1 3→4) **and** ramps ≈38 MTPA on
  schedule without net-debt blowing past 3x.

### Variant 4 — the trough is the trade; SAIL/Tata are mispriced *both ways*
- **Consensus:** "Avoid the high-beta PSUs/Europe-dragged names."
- **Variant [M]:** Their **high cyclical beta is the feature, not the bug — *if you buy them into
  losses.*** Tata and SAIL swing to outright losses at the bottom (devastating at peak entry) and
  mega-profits at the top (Tata ₹41,749cr, SAIL ₹12,243cr FY22). The mispricing: they look "cheap" on
  peak earnings × a low multiple (the classic cyclical trap) and "expensive"/loss-making at the trough
  (the actual entry point). *Net: own them at the trough loss, never at the peak P/E.* **[M]**

## The one-line thesis
**Indian steel is both a real per-capita-consumption structural runway AND a spread cycle hostage to
China — and the per-capita story is only capturable by the captive-ore, net-debt-disciplined,
value-added-tilted integrated winners. Own the cost-curve leader (JSW as the steadiest hold, Tata/JSPL
for captive-ore + turnaround torque) near a spread trough at/below replacement cost; buy into trough
losses, never peak earnings × a low multiple; avoid commodity re-rollers; treat China exports + the
steel spread as the trade signal and the per-capita runway as the hold thesis.**

*Answers the Research Question: yes — the through-cycle floor is structurally higher post-IBC, but the
real 10-yr data proves no name escapes the cycle (even JSW ran ROCE 5%→28%→8%). Entry discipline, not
name selection, is the dominant return driver.* **[H]**
