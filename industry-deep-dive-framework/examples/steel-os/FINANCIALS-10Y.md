## What the long-run data reveals (and how it updates the scorecards)

1. **This is THE textbook capital cycle — the entire steel thesis is in these four rows.** Tata Steel
   net profit went **-₹4,169cr (FY17) → +₹41,749cr (FY22) → -₹4,910cr (FY24)**, and ROCE **1% (FY15) →
   31% (FY22) → 7% (FY24)**. A ~30-point ROCE swing and a ₹46,000cr profit swing in one cycle. The
   investing rule writes itself: **buy at trough multiples / losses, sell into peak earnings — never
   the reverse.** Peak-FY22 earnings flattered every screen; the stocks de-rated as profits collapsed
   on schedule. **[H]**

2. **JSW Steel is the best operator through the cycle — least negative beta, fastest grower.** Its
   trough ROCE was 5% (FY16) vs negative for SAIL/JSPL, OPM never fell below ~11-13%, it avoided losses
   (sole exception: -₹481cr FY16), *and* it compounded sales fastest (~12% CAGR, ₹53k→₹185k cr). The
   efficient, captive-leaning, low-cost operator earns the cycle's premium and prints the milder
   drawdowns. If you must own steel across the whole cycle, this is the holding. **[H]**

3. **Tata Steel & SAIL = the highest cyclical beta — own them for the trough trade, not the hold.**
   Both swing to outright losses at the bottom (Tata FY15-17 & FY24; SAIL FY16-18) and to mega-profits
   at the top (Tata ₹41,749cr; SAIL ₹12,243cr, FY22). OPM halves peak-to-trough. Maximum torque to the
   commodity price — devastating if bought at peak earnings, explosive if bought into losses. **[H]**

4. **JSPL = the turnaround story.** Loss-making *every year FY15-20* (ROCE -1% to 5%, EPS as low as
   -₹32) while it deleveraged — interest fell ₹3,866cr (FY18) → ₹1,294cr (FY24) — then turned hard:
   +₹6,766cr profit and 24% ROCE in FY22, and OPM has since held a high floor (~18-20%). The classic
   "balance-sheet repair → re-rate" arc; the long-run data confirms the deleveraging, not just a single
   good year. **[M]**

5. **No structural compounder here — even the best operator is cyclical.** JSW's own ROCE still ran
   5% → 28% → 8% across the cycle. Steel rewards *entry discipline* over quality-and-hold. The right
   frame is the commodity cycle, not the franchise-compounder playbook. **[H]**
