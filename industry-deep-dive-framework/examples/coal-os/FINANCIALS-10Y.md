Coal India's ROCE is in a different universe: it spent a decade between **35% and 107%**. NLC

## What the long-run data reveals (and how it updates the thesis)

1. **Evidence:** Coal India's ROCE ran 35–107% across FY15–26, OPM hit ~33% (FY23–25), and net
   profit 5.3×'d from ₹7,038cr (FY18) to ₹37,369cr (FY24). **Interpretation:** these are not
   utility-like returns — they are franchise-monopoly returns on a low-capex, near-zero-debt asset
   base, sustained for a *decade*, through commodity cycles. **Conclusion:** the data strongly
   supports the **"cash-cow for longer than the market prices"** variant — Coal India earns its keep
   on Indian energy security (coal still ~70% of generation), and the durability of 40%+ ROCE through
   FY24 is hard to reconcile with a market that prices it as a terminal-decline asset. **[H]**

2. **Evidence:** But FY25→FY26ttm is the first real crack — ROCE **48%→35%**, OPM **33%→24%**,
   net profit **₹35,302cr→₹31,071cr (TTM)**. **Interpretation:** this is exactly the *shape* the
   bears predicted — realisation/volume softening compressing returns from the top. It is early and
   could be cyclical (e-auction premia, wage revision, one bad year), but it is *directionally* the
   melting-ice-cube signature. **Conclusion:** this is the **first genuine evidence for the "terminal
   decline" camp** — not yet decisive, but it moves the FY26 print into "watch closely" territory.
   **[M]**

3. **Evidence:** Coal India's dividend payout sat at 95–146% in FY15–18 and ~42–53% recently; capex
   is modest relative to the cash thrown off. **Interpretation:** management itself treats this as a
   harvest asset, not a growth compounder — returns the cash rather than chasing reinvestment.
   **Conclusion:** the **high-payout angle reinforces the cash-cow read** — a 6–8% dividend yield is a
   long runway of return-of-capital even if the terminal multiple never re-rates. The risk is *return
   of capital masking decline*, which is precisely why the FY26 softening matters. **[H]**

4. **Evidence:** NLC India earns ROCE ~7–13% (just 7% in FY24), with profit lumpy on tax/other-income
   swings (FY16 net profit collapsed to ₹68cr). **Interpretation:** lignite mining bolted to regulated
   power generation is a structurally *lower-return* business than Coal India's pure-play coal monopoly
   — the power leg dilutes the mining economics. **Conclusion:** NLC is **not a Coal India proxy**;
   it's a mid-single-digit-ROCE utility hybrid, attractive (if at all) on renewables build-out, not on
   coal cash economics. **[H]**

5. **Evidence:** NTPC delivers ROCE ~8–11% every single year FY15–26, with net profit compounding
   ₹9,992cr→₹27,546cr and OPM pinned ~22–31%. **Interpretation:** this is the regulated-utility model
   working as intended — assured RoE on a growing regulated asset base, low variance, no cycle.
   **Conclusion:** NTPC is the **"steady regulated utility"** anchor of the basket — you buy it for
   bond-like predictability and capacity growth (incl. its renewables arm), *not* for Coal India's
   outsized but now-softening returns. **[H]**

## Tie-back to the falsification test (coal.md)

The core debate in `coal.md` is **value-trap vs cash-cow** for Coal India. The falsification test was:
*if ROCE/OPM stay elevated, the cash-cow variant wins; if they roll over from the top, the
terminal-decline variant gets its first real evidence.* **The 10-year data delivers both signals at
once** — a decade of exceptional, durable returns (cash-cow) **and** a clear FY25→FY26ttm rollover
(first decline evidence). Net read: **cash-cow thesis still leads on the weight of evidence and the
dividend runway, but the falsification test is now partially triggered** — FY26 full-year ROCE/OPM is
the next decisive data point. NLC and NTPC do not face this test; they were never priced as cash cows.
