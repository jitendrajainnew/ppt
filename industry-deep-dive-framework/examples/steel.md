## ★ Research Question  [CORE] — do this BEFORE anything else
- **Central uncertainty:** *Is Indian steel a structural per-capita-consumption growth story with
  cost-curve winners — or a perennial value-destroyer hostage to Chinese oversupply and the capital
  cycle?*
- **Why it matters (the payoff):** the answer decides whether you own the integrated cost-curve
  leaders for a multi-decade volume compounding story, or trade the whole sector as a deep-cyclical
  spread/China play you must buy at the trough and sell at the peak.
- **PROVES it:** India per-capita consumption climbing toward world average, post-IBC discipline
  holding (no peak-capex blowups), captive-ore winners keeping a wide cost-curve gap, through-cycle
  EBITDA/tonne floor rising. **DISPROVES it:** China dumps and domestic spreads collapse anyway,
  the top names re-leverage into peak capacity, coking coal structurally crushes spreads.
- **One-line thesis (provisional):** the per-capita runway is real *but only the captive-ore,
  net-debt-disciplined, value-added-tilted integrated players capture it* — own the cost-curve
  winner near a spread trough, not commodity re-rollers or peak earnings.
- **Story type:** ☑ Structural growth (per-capita) + ☑ Cyclical turn (spread/China).
- **⏱ Horizon:** Investment (1–3y) overlaid with Structural (5–10y) _(most disagreements here are
  horizon mismatches — bulls quote 2040 per-capita, bears quote next quarter's China exports)_.

## 1. Why This Industry, Why Now  [CORE]
1. **India per-capita demand runway** — finished-steel consumption ≈ **100kg/capita** vs world avg
   ≈ **230** and China ≈ **600+**; India steel demand growing ≈ **8–10%/yr** on infra/auto/
   construction — the only large market still structurally *growing* volumes. **[M, verify]**
2. **China oversupply / dumping** — China ≈ **1.0bn t/yr (≈50%+ of world ≈1.9bn t)**; a property
   bust pushed China exports to ≈ **90–100Mt/yr**, flooding seaborne markets and undercutting
   Indian HRC — *the* swing risk and the reason spreads matter more than demand. **[H]**
3. **Capex super-cycle in India** — top names guiding toward ≈ **300+ MTPA national capacity by
   2030** (from ≈ **150 MTPA**); the *timing* of this capex (trough vs peak) is the whole capital-
   allocation question. **[M]**
4. **Spreads, not demand, move the P&L** — the **steel spread** (HRC price minus iron-ore + coking-
   coal cost) swung EBITDA/tonne from ≈ **₹15,000+ at the 2021 peak to ≈₹5,000–8,000** in the
   trough; the cycle, not volume, drives earnings. **[H]**
5. **Green-steel / CBAM clock** — EU **CBAM** (carbon border tax) phases in from ≈2026, hydrogen-DRI
   and decarbonisation capex looms — a trade-barrier risk to exports and a cost-inflation force
   that reshapes the long-run cost curve. **[M]**

## 2. What It Is & History  [CORE — keep brief]
- **5-year-old test:** you dig up iron ore (rust-coloured rock) and bake it with coal in a giant
  furnace to make liquid iron, then burn out the carbon to make steel — the metal in cars, buildings,
  bridges and railways. India makes ≈150Mt/yr; China makes ≈half the world's.
- **Origin event for India:** **Tata Steel at Jamshedpur (1907)** — Asia's first integrated steel
  plant; the public sector (SAIL/Bhilai, 1950s–60s) built the licence-raj base.

| Era / Year | What happened | Why it matters today |
|---|---|---|
| 1907 / 1950s–60s | Tata (1907) private; SAIL/Bhilai (Nehru era) public-sector build | Set the integrated, captive-ore legacy base |
| Pre-1991 | Licence-raj; capacity controlled, SAIL/Tata duopoly-ish | Suppressed private scale; under-built vs need |
| **1991 liberalisation** | De-licensing → private entry (JSW, JSPL, Essar, Bhushan, Ispat) | Built the modern private champions |
| **2000s capex boom** | Debt-funded greenfield rush at peak prices (2004–2012) | **Sowed the NPA bust — the core cautionary tale** |
| **2014–18 NPA bust** | China dumping + over-leverage → mass defaults (Essar, Bhushan, Electrosteel, Monnet, Lanco) | Made *balance-sheet survival* the dominant cyclical risk |
| **2017–19 IBC consolidation** | Bankruptcy code → distressed assets sold to strong hands (Tata→Bhushan, AM/NS→Essar, JSW→Bhushan Power/Monnet) | **Consolidated the top-5; raised the cost-curve floor** |
| 2020 → | China-driven cycles (COVID bust → 2021 super-spread → China-export glut); captive-ore auctions; green/CBAM | The current spread-cycle + consolidation thesis |

*Evidence:* the timeline above. *Interpretation:* the 2000s peak-capex boom built the NPA bust;
IBC then handed those assets to disciplined operators. *Conclusion:* history explains the structure —
**capacity timing + a fortress balance sheet, not technology, decide who survives the cycle.** **[H]**

## 3. How It's Made — *ore to coil*  [CORE]  (cost stack + tech roadmap over-weighted per archetype)
- **Process flow (two routes):**
  - **BF-BOF (integrated, ≈45–50% of India, ≈70%+ globally):** **iron ore + coking coal (→ coke)** →
    **blast furnace** (liquid hot metal) → **basic oxygen furnace** (blow out carbon → crude steel) →
    **continuous casting** (slabs/billets/blooms) → **hot rolling → HRC** → cold rolling / coating
    (CRC, galvanised) **or** long-product mills (rebar/wire-rod). Lowest cash cost *if* ore + coal
    are captive; coking-coal-import-exposed.
  - **DRI-EAF / scrap route (≈50% of India via secondary, growing):** **iron ore + natural gas or
    coal → DRI (sponge iron)** *or* **scrap** → **electric arc / induction furnace** → crude steel →
    casting → rolling. Lower capex, more flexible, lower carbon — but scrap-price and power-cost
    exposed; India is scrap-short.
- **Cost stack [illustrative BF-BOF, verify]:**
  | Input | ≈% of cost | Sourcing risk |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Iron ore** | ≈25–35% | Captive vs merchant (NMDC/auction price); the cost-curve swing |
  | **Coking coal** | ≈30–40% | **≈85%+ imported (Australia)** — the biggest external cost shock |
  | Energy / power / fuel | ≈10–15% | Power tariff, captive-power advantage |
  | Conversion (labour, refractories, fluxes, O&M) | ≈15–20% | Operating-leverage on utilisation |
  | Freight / logistics | ≈5–10% | Pithead/port location; rail/road evacuation |
- **Core technology & where it's heading (next-gen):** the roadmap is **decarbonisation** — BF-BOF is
  ≈2t CO₂/t steel; the transition runs BF-BOF → **scrap-EAF** (low-carbon, scrap-constrained in India)
  → **green hydrogen-DRI** (zero-carbon iron, capex-heavy, hydrogen-cost-gated) and **CCUS** retrofits.
  **CBAM (EU carbon border tax, phasing from 2026)** prices the carbon at the border → a structural
  cost-inflation + trade-barrier force. *The decarbonisation capex reshapes the long-run cost curve
  and could strand high-carbon BF capacity — but is gated on hydrogen economics, a 2030s+ swing.* **[M]**

## 5. Value Chain & Margin Map ⭐  [CORE]  (THE COST CURVE — the central act)
**Chain:** **Iron ore (mine)** → **Coking coal / coke** → **Iron-making (BF / DRI)** → **Steel-making
(BOF / EAF)** → **Casting → Rolling (flat/long)** → **Value-added (coating/auto-grade)** →
Distribution → End user (auto / construction / infra).

| Stage | Margin | Capex | Competition | Recurring? | **Who captures it** | Margin ↑/↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Iron ore (captive mine)** | **High** | High | Low (auction-gated) | Yes | **NMDC / SAIL / Tata (captive)** | → high |
| Coking coal | Pass-through cost | n/a (mostly imported) | Seaborne | No | Australian miners (BHP etc.) | volatile |
| **Iron-making (BF/DRI)** | Mid–High | **Very High** | Low (scale-gated) | Yes | Integrated majors | → |
| Steel-making (BOF/EAF) | Mid | Very High | Med | Yes | Integrated majors | → |
| Rolling — **flat (HRC)** | **Good** | High | Lower (few mills) | Yes | Tata / JSW / AM-NS | ↑ value-added |
| Rolling — **long (commodity)** | **Thin/eroding** | Med | Very high (re-rollers) | Yes | Fragmented; price-takers | ↓ |
| **Value-added / auto-grade** | **High** | High | Low (qualified suppliers) | Sticky | Tata / JSW / AM-NS auto | ↑ |
| Distribution / service centres | Low–Mid | Low | High | Semi | Distributors / OEMs | → |

- **Max value captured:** **captive iron ore + value-added/auto-grade flat**. **Value destroyed:**
  non-integrated commodity-long re-rollers (no ore, no moat, China-undercut first). **[H]**
- **The cost-curve insight (the whole game):** *Evidence:* iron ore is ≈25–35% of cost and coking
  coal ≈30–40%; captive ore can be ≈₹2,000–4,000/t cheaper than buying from NMDC/auction at peak.
  *Interpretation:* the player with **captive iron ore + captive power + scale** sits at the **bottom
  of the cost curve** and survives any spread; the non-integrated buyer is **margin-squeezed first**.
  *Conclusion:* **cost-curve position — driven mainly by captive-ore % — is the only durable moat in a
  commodity; it is the single most important variable in this deep dive.** **[H]**
- **Who is integrating (back/forward) & why:** majors integrating **backward into iron-ore mines via
  auctions** (Tata, JSW bidding for blocks; SAIL/NMDC already captive) — to escape merchant-ore cost
  and secure supply; some integrating into **captive coking coal / overseas met-coal** (limited,
  geology-constrained); forward into **value-added/coating + distribution** to capture the spread
  above commodity HRC. **JSW is the notable *non-captive-ore* major — it buys most ore — its
  structural cost-curve gap.** **[H]**

## 5c. Red Flag Checklist ⭐  [CORE — the one-page kill-criteria sheet]
> The *inverse* of the scorecard: industry-specific signs a steel name is a trap. If any fire, dig
> before you buy.
| # | Red flag (industry-specific) | Why it kills value | Where to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High net-debt/EBITDA heading into a downturn (>3x)** | Trough EBITDA can't service debt → distress/dilution/IBC | Balance sheet, debt schedule, interest cover |
| 2 | **No captive raw material (no ore, all merchant coking coal)** | Sits high on the cost curve → first squeezed when spreads compress | Captive-ore %, ore-sourcing notes, mine leases |
| 3 | **Capacity expansion commissioned at peak prices** | Capex paid at the top → returns crushed when the cycle turns | Capex timeline vs spread history, ordering dates |
| 4 | **Single-product / commodity-grade exposure (rebar re-roller)** | No pricing power, no value-added; China dumps into it first | Product mix, VAP %, flat-vs-long split |
| 5 | **China-import undercutting domestic realisation** | Imports cap domestic prices regardless of demand → spread collapse | China export MT, landed-import vs domestic HRC |
| 6 | **Working-capital blowout (inventory/receivables ballooning)** | Cash trapped in a falling-price inventory → liquidity crunch | WC days, inventory at cost vs falling NRV |

- **Concentration:** ☐ Oligopoly ☑ **Consolidating** (post-IBC top-5) ☐ Fragmented (the long/secondary tail)
- **Porter's 5:** Rivalry **High** (commodity, vs China imports) · Entrants **Low** (huge capex + ore
  + clearances) · Substitutes **Low–Med** (aluminium/composites at the margin) · Supplier **High**
  (≈85% imported coking coal; merchant-ore power) · **Buyer Med–High** (auto OEMs concentrated &
  price-pressing; construction fragmented).
- **Where know-how / barriers sit / key global players:** the moat is **resource ownership (captive
  ore) + scale + low-cost geology/logistics + auto-grade qualification**, not IP. Key global players:
  **ArcelorMittal** (world's largest ex-China), **Nippon Steel**, **POSCO**, **Baowu (China, #1 by
  volume)**; seaborne raw materials set by **BHP / Rio / Vale (iron ore)** and **BHP / Teck
  (coking coal)**.

## 8. Demand, Supply & Cycle  [CORE]  (THE capital-cycle classic — over-weighted per archetype)
- **Demand drivers:** India = **infra (roads/rail/ports) + auto + construction/real-estate +
  appliances**; structurally growing on the per-capita gap (≈100kg vs world ≈230). Long products
  track construction/infra; flat tracks auto/manufacturing. China demand **rolling over** on the
  property bust — the global swing.
- **Supply situation:** India ramping toward ≈300MTPA by 2030; **China's ≈1bn t of capacity is the
  global overhang** — when domestic Chinese demand falls, that steel exports out and floods seaborne.
- **Cycle position:** ☑ **Mid-cycle, spreads off the 2021 super-peak, pressured by China exports
  [M]** — HRC well below the 2021 highs, coking coal volatile, spreads compressed but above trough.
- **Demand cycle vs STOCK cycle (what really moves the stocks):** *Evidence:* India *volume* demand is
  steadily growing (≈8–10%), yet steel stocks are violently cyclical. *Interpretation:* what moves the
  *stocks* is the **steel spread + China export headlines + net-debt trajectory**, not domestic volume.
  *Conclusion:* **trade the spread/China/capital cycle, not the demand growth story — the volume
  runway is the *structural* leg, the spread is the *trade*.** **[H]**
- **★ Second-Order Effects (chase the chain):**
  - **China property bust → domestic Chinese steel demand falls → mills keep running (state/employment)
    → surplus exports out → global HRC collapses → Indian domestic prices capped via import parity →
    spreads compress for *all* Indian players regardless of local demand.** The under-appreciated node:
    *India's demand growth doesn't protect spreads — China's export valve does.* **[H]**
  - **Infra/auto/construction demand split → long (construction) vs flat (auto) diverge → value-added
    flat holds margin while commodity long gets China-dumped → the *mix* (flat/VAP) decides who
    survives the same spread.** The opportunity sits in **value-added flat**, not headline volume. **[M]**
  - **Green-steel mandate (CBAM) → decarbonisation capex (hydrogen-DRI) → cost inflation + new trade
    barriers → high-carbon BF capacity at risk of stranding / CBAM tax on exports → a 2030s cost-curve
    re-rank.** The 2–3-steps-down node: *who decarbonises cheapest (captive scrap/gas + green power)
    wins the next cost curve.* **[M]**
- **Key numbers [illustrative ~FY24, verify]:** world crude steel ≈ **1.9bn t**; China ≈ **1.0bn t
  (≈50%+)**; India ≈ **140–150Mt (world #2)**, demand growing ≈ **8–10%**; China steel exports ≈
  **90–100Mt/yr**; India per-capita ≈ **100kg** vs world ≈ **230**, China ≈ **600+**; EBITDA/tonne
  swung ≈ **₹15,000+ (2021 peak) → ₹5,000–8,000 (trough)**; coking coal ≈ **85%+ imported**. **[M, verify]**

## 9. Regulation, Policy & Government  [CORE]
| Policy / rule / body | Effect | Tailwind or risk? |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron-ore mining auctions (MMDR Act)** | Captive blocks allocated by auction; sets ore-cost edge | **Cost-curve swing** (winners get cheap captive ore) |
| **Export / import duties (steel)** | Govt toggles to manage prices/inflation (e.g. 2022 export duty) | Two-sided risk (policy whiplash) |

## 16. Valuation, Verdict & Investment Fit  [CORE]
- **Valuation method & why:** *explicitly a cyclical — buy at trough multiples on mid-cycle earnings,
  never at peak earnings on a low multiple (the classic cyclical trap).* Primary frame:
  **EV/EBITDA on *mid-cycle* (normalised) EBITDA/tonne**, cross-checked with **EV/tonne vs
  replacement cost** (≈$700–1,000/t of capacity is a rough new-build benchmark — buying below
  replacement is the margin of safety) and **P/B at cycle extremes** (cheap steel bottoms near/below
  book, tops at rich multiples on peak earnings). *Margin-of-safety check:* never capitalise peak
  spreads; haircut to a normalised spread and test EV/tonne vs replacement cost.
- **Read:** **own the cost-curve winner near a spread trough below replacement cost; the commodity
  tail is a value-trap.** Peak earnings × a low P/E = the most dangerous-looking "cheap" in markets. **[M]**
